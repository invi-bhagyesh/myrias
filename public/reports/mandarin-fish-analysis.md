# Mandarin fish: should feed transition advance as a welfare opportunity?

Preliminary analysis | 17 September 2026

GPT-4o via OpenRouter; edited by Codex against the supplied source notes. Not independently expert reviewed. Decision: investigate a specified formulation and transition protocol; insufficient evidence to recommend general replacement of live prey on welfare grounds.

## Executive judgment

Feed transition is a suitable research case because the assessment must account for several animal populations. A benefit to prey fish cannot simply be assumed to outweigh a health cost to mandarin fish, and formulated feed may introduce other animal inputs. The immediate decision is to define and investigate a bounded intervention, not to assign an overall welfare score or recommend adoption. This is an analyst judgment from a limited packet, not an estimated net effect.

## Scope and comparator

The starting comparison is a specified artificial feed against live prey for juvenile Siniperca chuatsi. Commercial deployment would additionally require a named strain, life stage, production system, geography, formulation and transition procedure. These parameters have not been resolved for a Myrias intervention. Long-term grow-out, farm adoption and supply-chain welfare remain open questions rather than exclusions from the eventual assessment.

## Evidence ledger

M1 — Fang et al. (2024), Abstract, Animal feeding experiment, Table 1 and Results. The study assigned 480 fish, initially about 25.1 g, to 12 tanks, six per diet, for eight weeks. The authors report changes in muscle glucose/lipid metabolism, increased malondialdehyde and pro-inflammatory gene expression under the tested artificial diet. The formula included fish meal, krill powder, squid offal and fish oil. These findings identify a formulation-specific physiological concern; they do not quantify subjective suffering or establish that all artificial feeds have the same effects. ## Welfare pathways and affected animals

Analyst framework proposal: keep three separate accounting records. First, target fish: characterize feeding acceptance, injury, disease, behavior and recovery over a defined period. Second, live-prey animals: establish the number displaced and the relevant rearing, transport and predation exposures. Third, replacement-feed animals: identify ingredient origins, quantities and counterfactual uses. None of these supply-chain quantities is estimated here.

For every population, record severity, duration, prevalence and the basis for connecting observed endpoints to experience. Preserve sentience assumptions separately. Population size is an impact input, not evidence of intervention efficacy. No total benefit, business-case score or promisingness score is defensible from this packet.

## Implementation and business case

Proposed participants are a farm operator, feed supplier, nutrition researcher and welfare reviewer; these are roles to recruit, not confirmed partners. The operator would need usable feeding instructions, monitoring capacity and predefined response rules. The supplier would need to disclose the relevant formulation and ingredient provenance.

Collect feed cost per unit of harvested output, labor, adaptation losses, survival, growth, veterinary inputs and monitoring costs in a named currency and year. Potential savings remain hypotheses. Establish current adoption and what would happen without Myrias before claiming additionality. A plausible backfire is pursuing rapid replacement while overlooking poor feed acceptance or shifting animal harm elsewhere in the supply chain.

## Decision and next research

1. Resolve one intervention/comparator pair and retrieve the complete study record. A methods reviewer should check experimental units, missing data and the relevance of endpoints before evidence extraction is promoted.
2. Prepare a farm-and-supplier feasibility interview with ingredient disclosure and current-practice questions. Stop broad impact calculations if animal-input quantities cannot be bounded.
3. Design, for expert review, a monitored comparison with behavioral and clinical outcomes alongside physiology. Specify recovery monitoring and welfare stopping rules before any live-animal work; this report does not authorize or define a trial protocol.
4. Reassess only after target-fish outcomes and displaced-animal pathways can be compared. Consistent evidence of adverse outcomes would oppose advancing that formulation. Evidence of acceptable target-fish outcomes and a credible positive multi-population balance could support pilot review.

## Limitations and next update

One selected study is not a literature review. No pooled effect, prevalence estimate, farm budget or deployment recommendation is supplied. The research priority reflects a decision-relevant uncertainty, not a demonstrated high-value intervention. The next version should incorporate competing formulations, behavior-focused evidence and supply-chain data. Proposed judgments remain outside the accepted graph pending review.
